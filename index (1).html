SECTION 01 • BACKGROUND & PURPOSE

INTRODUCTION

Research Background

The study focuses on developing and evaluating a prototype that utilizes saltwater, specifically aqueous sodium chloride, to generate electricity for emergency lighting and limited mobile-phone charging.

Addresses brownouts & disaster power interruptions
Accessible, low-cost, portable backup energy

Research Objectives

  1. 01 Develop a saltwater-powered emergency lamp and mobile charging station.
  2. 02 Measure the electrical output of the prototype.
  3. 03 Determine the operating duration of the emergency lamp.
  4. 04 Evaluate its mobile-phone charging capability.
  5. 05 Explore its potential as an alternative energy source during power interruptions.

SECTION 04 • SYNTHESIS OF FINDINGS

CONCLUSION

KEY RESEARCH TAKEAWAY

The S.A.I.L. prototype demonstrated the potential of saltwater-assisted electrochemical cells as an alternative source of energy for emergency use.

Summary of Key Findings

Measurable Electrical Output

The prototype generated measurable voltage (0.75–0.85 V) and power output (0.00556–0.00745 W).

294.67 Minutes Lamp Runtime

The emergency lamp operated for an average of 294.67 minutes (~4 hours and 55 minutes) before automatic 11.5 V cutoff.

+6% Mobile Charging in 30 Min

Mobile-phone charging showed an average battery percentage increase of 6% in 30 minutes.

Emergency Application Potential

The system demonstrated potential for emergency lighting and low-power communication support.

IMPORTANT LIMITATION

Scope of Power Capacity

The prototype cannot yet replace conventional electricity or support high-power appliances because of its limited electrical output.

Intended strictly as an experimental low-power emergency backup system.
